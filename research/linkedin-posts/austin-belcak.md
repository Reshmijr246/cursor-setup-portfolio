# Austin Belcak

**Category:** LinkedIn Profile Optimization, Career Authority & Audience-Led Content

**LinkedIn Profile:** https://www.linkedin.com/in/abelcak/

**Why Selected:**  
Austin Belcak is the founder of Cultivated Culture and one of LinkedIn's most visible career-content operators, with roughly 1.49M followers and 2,700+ posts visible in public LinkedIn metadata. He is especially relevant to this repository because his content model turns career advice into practical, high-engagement LinkedIn posts: promotion strategy, hiring-market commentary, profile optimization, networking, career-change systems, and tool-led CTAs. While his primary audience is job seekers rather than B2B SaaS buyers, the mechanics are highly transferable to SaaS founder, executive, and employee advocacy programs: teach a narrow audience, give tactical steps in-feed, make value visible, and use the profile/comments/tools as the next step.

## Post 1

**Date:** June 4, 2026

**Post URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/abelcak_7-principles-to-get-promoted-in-2026-with-activity-7468649329860464640-dqAA

**Summary:**  
Belcak shares seven principles for winning more promotions and larger raises in 2026. The post argues that professionals often assume harder work alone will earn advancement, but the bigger lever is clarity and visibility: know the role/team you want, tell your manager, set regular progress check-ins, communicate goals beyond the direct manager, build social capital with people who influence promotion decisions, overdeliver against measurable expectations, and prepare before the formal promotion cycle. He closes with a client example of moving into a 100% remote Fortune 500 role with a $50,000 raise and points readers toward a free career clarity call.

**Key Takeaway:**  
Promotion content maps cleanly to B2B LinkedIn organic strategy: great work is not enough if decision-makers cannot see it. For SaaS teams, organic content should make customer impact, internal expertise, and executive point of view visible before buying or advocacy decisions are made.

## Post 2

**Date:** May 23, 2026

**Post URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/abelcak_i-saw-this-post-on-reddit-last-week-ive-activity-7464300675489886208-e39z

**Summary:**  
Belcak references a Reddit post and argues that companies should stop asking candidates to explain employment gaps. He frames the practice as outdated and biased, especially after widespread layoffs: if companies laid off millions of workers, it is inconsistent to later penalize candidates for resume gaps created by the same market. The post states that a candidate's value does not disappear because of a gap; employers should evaluate skills, proof, and qualifications instead of using timeline breaks as a shortcut filter.

**Key Takeaway:**  
Strong LinkedIn organic content often takes a clear stance on an industry norm. For B2B SaaS, this is a useful pattern: identify a broken practice your audience recognizes, name the contradiction, and make the argument in plain language that invites discussion.

## Post 3

**Date:** May 3, 2026

**Post URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/abelcak_the-25-post-strategy-for-easily-changing-activity-7457113316474195968-GG_F

**Summary:**  
Belcak explains the "25 Post Strategy" for changing careers and building experience in a new field. The method is simple: choose a publishing medium such as LinkedIn, Medium, or a blog; pick a focus aligned with the new field; set a 30-60 day timeline; commit to 25 posts; create and share consistently; and use the process to learn in public while becoming visible to people in the target space. The strategy is framed as asymmetric: the best case is that public sharing creates opportunities, while the worst case is that the writer builds real knowledge and proof of interest.

**Key Takeaway:**  
Learning in public is a practical organic growth engine. B2B SaaS teams can adapt the 25-post model for founders, product marketers, sales leaders, and subject-matter experts entering a category or trying to build category authority.

Content & Distribution Model:
- Core platform: LinkedIn personal profile
- Primary content type: short tactical posts with strong hooks, practical frameworks, and direct reader application

- Audience loop: post teaches a career problem, comments reinforce or debate the idea, and CTAs capture higher intent
- Proof model: career transformation stories, quantified raises, examples from job seekers, and repeatable workflows
- Format discipline: list posts, contrarian stance posts, before/after logic, clear steps, and scannable paragraphs

Potential Playbook Contributions:
- Visibility-first content system: document work, outcomes, and stakeholder value before decisions happen
- Promotion/readiness content template: principle list + tactical actions + proof story + low-friction CTA
- Contrarian norm-challenge post: call out an outdated practice, explain why it is unfair or inefficient, then restate the better evaluation standard
- 25-post learning-in-public sprint: publish 25 category-specific posts in 30-60 days to build competence and visibility
- Saveable framework template: define the problem, give 5-8 steps, include a concrete example, end with a clear next step
- Profile-as-funnel checklist: headline, Featured section, offer/resource links, proof, recommendations, and clear next step
- Comment CTA strategy: keep the post useful on its own, then add tool links or deeper resources in comments
- Employee advocacy onboarding: require profile readiness before asking employees to post regularly
- Founder/operator content cadence: prioritize consistent publishing and audience feedback over algorithm chasing
- Product-led organic motion: turn high-intent problems into free graders, calculators, analyzers, and templates promoted through posts
