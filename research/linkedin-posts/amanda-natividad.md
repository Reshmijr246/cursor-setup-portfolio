## Post 1

**Date:** June 11, 2026

**Post URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/amandanat_when-google-stops-sending-clicks-what-still-activity-7470490713688850432-RXwC

**Summary:**  
Natividad shares SparkToro's latest research finding that 68% of U.S. Google searches now end without a click—to any destination—and reframes the conversation away from "is SEO dead?" toward a new scoreboard. She argues traffic was always a proxy metric, not the goal, and that as Google AI Overviews, social feeds, and AI tools filter and summarize before users reach websites, marketers must become the brand people and machines look up by name. The post teases a SparkToro blog breakdown covering who still wins Google's remaining clicks, why AI-referred visitors may convert better despite lower volume, and three durable bets: branded search as moat, owned assets (email, community, first-party data), and correlation dashboards over click-based attribution. She pairs the written piece with a bonus *Zero Click Marketing* podcast Field Notes episode for audio-first consumption.

**Key Takeaway:**  
When platform traffic declines structurally—not tactically—B2B SaaS teams should shift LinkedIn and organic programs from click-chasing to brand-building: publish native insights that compound recognition, convert rented attention into owned newsletters, and measure branded search and revenue correlation instead of session counts.

## Post 2

**Date:** June 6, 2026

**Post URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/amandanat_software-developers-use-google-less-than-activity-7468319420340191232-iQDa

**Summary:**  
Using SparkToro audience research, Natividad reports that software developers at startups use Google less than the average American (~82% vs. ~95%) and over-index on alternative discovery channels: Claude (~4× average usage), Perplexity, Brave, DeepSeek, and—surprisingly—GitHub as a bigger "social network" for this cohort than LinkedIn. She positions the marketing implication for B2B SaaS teams selling to technical buyers: ranking first on Google matters less than showing up accurately when a developer asks an AI assistant about your category. The LinkedIn post drives to a full SparkToro playbook ([*How We'd Market to Software Developers at Startups*](https://sparktoro.com/blog/how-wed-market-to-software-developers-at-startups/)) covering channel fit, GitHub as a trust surface, and AI answer visibility as a new marketing KPI.

**Key Takeaway:**  
Audience research should precede channel strategy—LinkedIn may not be where your ICP actually discovers vendors; B2B SaaS marketers should audit AI answer accuracy and developer-native surfaces (GitHub, technical communities) alongside traditional SEO and executive LinkedIn programs.

## Post 3

**Date:** May 23, 2026

**Post URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/amandanat_one-of-the-most-interesting-challenges-in-activity-7463265297563541504-EbDs

**Summary:**  
Natividad frames double-sided marketplace marketing—platforms serving both vendors and end users (The Knot, Thumbtack, Clutch)—as a trust problem with two audiences and one shared emotional driver. She uses SparkToro data to show homeowners search by problem ("roof leak repair," "licensed contractors near me") while service pros search for leads ("get leads online," "contractor marketing tools"), yet both sides need confidence in the other. The post links to a SparkToro deep dive ([*How Do You Market to a Double-Sided Marketplace?*](https://sparktoro.com/blog/how-do-you-market-to-a-double-sided-marketplace/)) with audience-specific messaging, distribution recommendations (YouTube, Reddit, contractor podcasts), and content that serves one side while strengthening marketplace trust overall.

**Key Takeaway:**  
Multi-audience B2B SaaS platforms (marketplaces, partner ecosystems, buyer + seller tools) need separate messaging per side anchored on a shared trust narrative—LinkedIn content should address distinct jobs-to-be-done rather than one generic "platform" pitch.

## Research Notes

Recurring Themes:
- Zero-click content: standalone value on-platform without requiring website visits

Potential Playbook Contributions:
- Zero-click LinkedIn post template: standalone insight, no link in body, CTA to search brand by name or visit profile
- Algorithm capital cadence: 4–5 native-value posts per 1 link post (newsletter signup, demo, blog)
- Audience research → content pipeline: run SparkToro (or equivalent) report → extract 3 surprising data points → LinkedIn teaser → long-form asset
- Multi-format repurposing loop: LinkedIn post → blog → podcast episode → newsletter edition
- Branded search + correlation dashboard KPIs for organic programs (branded search volume, direct traffic, revenue movement)
- AI answer audit checklist for B2B SaaS: prompt category queries in Claude/Perplexity/ChatGPT; verify accuracy of brand representation
- Channel-fit matrix by ICP: map where audience over-indexes before committing LinkedIn budget
- Double-sided marketplace messaging split: problem-first (demand side) vs. lead-quality (supply side), unified by trust
- Newsletter growth via LinkedIn: tease one insight, deliver full value in owned email (*The Menu* / SparkToro model)
- Employee advocacy without links: profile-header CTAs + native posts (Dreamdata pattern)
