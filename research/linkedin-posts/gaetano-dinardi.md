## Post 1

**Date:** May 30, 2026

**Post URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/officialg_organic-traffic-is-still-worth-tracking-activity-7465012640856133633--aid

**Summary:**  
DiNardi responds to an industry debate (sparked by Peter Rota) with a Search Engine Land article arguing organic traffic is still worth tracking—but only when filtered to revenue-influencing pages. He defines four reporting slices B2B SaaS teams should isolate: homepage (brand vs. non-brand split), pricing/demo pages (direct buying signals), product/solutions pages (steady mixed-intent traffic), and "money" content (comparisons, teardowns, bottom-of-funnel pages that skew non-branded). He contends most SaaS sites carry reporting noise—expired, low-value pages that inflate traffic dashboards without influencing buyers—and that filtering TOFU clutter makes organic performance legible again.

**Key Takeaway:**  
B2B SaaS organic programs should report traffic by revenue-page cohort, not site-wide sessions; teams still defending aggregate organic traffic numbers are optimizing for a metric that masks whether SEO is actually filling pipeline.

## Post 2

**Date:** May 23, 2026

**Post URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/officialg_if-youre-a-challenger-brand-going-up-against-activity-7463258679061037056-sQ_f

**Summary:**  
Using Ahrefs Agent A, DiNardi benchmarks a challenger in AI access management against incumbent Saviynt and finds a 9× organic traffic gap driven less by domain rating (71 vs. 65) than by page inventory—Saviynt holds hundreds of top-10 ranking pages (product pages, 50+ glossary entries, comparison pages, analyst coverage) while the challenger has under 10. He decomposes the gap: ~50% page inventory (6 months to close), ~25% branded search demand (12–24 months of PR, analyst briefings, podcasts), ~10% backlink-driven DR (6–12 months digital PR), ~10% homepage/product optimization (fixable quickly), ~5% technical hygiene (fixable immediately)—concluding realistic competitive parity takes ~24 months. The post models data-backed expectation-setting for challenger-brand organic acquisition.

**Key Takeaway:**  
B2B SaaS startups cannot shortcut organic acquisition against category incumbents with DR hacks alone; sustainable competitive SEO requires multi-year investment in page inventory, branded demand, and surround-sound visibility—not a 90-day content sprint.

## Post 3

**Date:** May 16, 2026

**Post URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/officialg_the-attribution-black-box-is-going-to-disproportionately-activity-7460748401790427137-WuAF

**Summary:**  
DiNardi shares a "SEO Value Chain" diagram from CRO/RevOps leadership and critiques its linear, ranking-and-click assumptions in an AI/zero-click era. He highlights a structural unfairness: SEO reporting often filters out branded organic conversions (homepage, pricing), while PPC celebrates high-ROI branded search campaigns—creating a double standard where SEO gets scrutinized for non-branded ROI and paid search "proves" value by bidding on demand other channels created (citing Rand Fishkin's "promo flyers to hungry people walking into the pizza shop" analogy). He links to his Substack on the Dark SEO funnel and recommends Amanda Natividad and Rand Fishkin's *Zero-Click Marketing* as the overarching narrative for marketers facing invisible attribution.

**Key Takeaway:**  
B2B SaaS teams evaluating organic channel ROI must redesign attribution to credit branded search lift and LLM-driven discovery—or SEO budgets will lose to PPC's trackable (but often recycled) demand while the channel that actually built awareness gets defunded.

## Research Notes

Recurring Themes:
- Revenue over traffic: "Do you get measured on traffic or pipeline and revenue?" as the strategic forcing function
- Bottom-of-funnel / money keywords over TOFU definitional content ("What is demand generation?" traps)
- Dark SEO funnel: discovery in LLMs → branded Google verification → conversion credited to direct/branded search
- The Great Normalization: trading high-volume informational traffic for high-intent signal (traffic ↓, demo CVR ↑, pipeline →)
- Attribution asymmetry: SEO punished on non-branded ROI; PPC rewarded for branded search capture
- Challenger realism: ~24 months to compete with entrenched incumbents; page inventory is ~50% of the gap
- AI search as secondary benefit of differentiation, PR, and great content—not a first channel for unpositioned startups
- Surround sound SEO: third-party mentions across review sites, communities, and publishers drive LLM recommendations
- Barbecue content: original insights and unique perspective that LLMs cannot synthesize from stable knowledge
- Filtered organic reporting: homepage, pricing/demo, products/solutions, money content—exclude noise pages
- Anti–programmatic SEO: rejection of glossary farms, SEO heists, and HubSpot-inbound copycat playbooks
- AI executes tasks; humans own the channel (judgment, prioritization, accountability when numbers tank)
- Founder-led marketing and LinkedIn as distribution for frameworks that drive advisory waitlist and Substack growth
